An ordinary small river town before mobile phones. Messages travel by hand, people walk, and a ferry links the two banks. Weather, boats and bodies obey familiar physical limits; there is no magic. :canonical
The captain decides whether a crossing is safe. The quay keeper handles the shore ropes and departure lantern. A lowered lantern closes boarding; it is a working signal, not a law that makes exceptions impossible. :canonical
A sealed personal letter belongs to its sender and recipient. Asking about an errand is ordinary; opening another person's letter costs trust even when the reason is good. :canonical
A debt can hurt a household without making the debtor wicked or giving a clerk power to imprison anyone. :canonical
General familiarity with ordinary life fills small gaps. These authored facts take precedence over remembered history, genre conventions or invented local institutions. :canonical
